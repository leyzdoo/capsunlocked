THE SEASON / NOVEMBER 13, 2026

The next pass.

Shantay Legans has more ways to score. Can the Pilots make their good habits last?

Caps Unlocked · Analysis based on the linked reporting below · Pregame edition ·

Shantay Legans’s sixth season at Portland begins with a useful distinction: scoring is easier to notice than progress. The Pilots have opened 3–1, won a tournament in Hawaii and put up some startling offensive numbers. The harder question is whether they can protect the ball, defend through a scoring drought and stay healthy long enough to make those performances repeatable.

The strongest evidence came in Portland’s 73–66 road win at Hawaii. The Pilots struggled to score but held the Rainbow Warriors scoreless for more than nine minutes in the second half. Kaia Isaac’s free throws, a Jaxon Olvera steal and a Riley Parker three helped settle the game. For a coach who wants pace, passing and plenty of threes, winning without a steady supply of baskets matters.

Legans learned the game as a point guard. Diego Sandoval’s Noozhawk profile follows him through California, Fresno State and professional basketball in the Netherlands, tracing a career built despite doubts about his size. Coaching followed: high school work, eight seasons as an Eastern Washington assistant and then four as head coach. His Portland biography records a 75–49 head-coaching mark at Eastern and a 2021 NCAA Tournament appearance. He arrived on The Bluff that spring.

His biggest Portland win came last February, when the Pilots beat No. 6 Gonzaga, 87–80. Gonzaga’s recap records Joel Foxwell’s 27 points and the end of the Bulldogs’ fifteen-game winning streak. It showed Portland’s ceiling. The season around it showed how difficult reaching that level regularly could be, particularly with injuries thinning the rotation.

Foxwell is now at Arizona State. His current biography lists what Portland must replace: 15.6 points and 6.5 assists per game, including a school-record 221 assists. If you remember an excellent point guard from last season, that is him. His younger brother Austin is a freshman on this roster. This year’s offense, though, is designed to spread the work more widely.

In Kyle Garcia’s season-opening feature, Legans explains how returning players let him recruit for fit. Last season’s injuries pushed the offense toward Foxwell’s individual creation. This group offers more ways to start and finish a possession. Watch whether a pass produces another quick decision: a shot, a drive or a second pass. Moving the ball has value when somebody uses the opening it creates.

James “Jimbo” O’Donnell gives that movement a destination inside. The Sydney forward was Rainbow Classic MVP after averaging 19.7 points and 11.7 rebounds across three games, including 29 points and 20 rebounds against Bethesda. His signing announcement traces his route through Australia’s Centre of Excellence and San Francisco. Timo George supplies another interior option after a shoulder injury cut short his freshman season. Together, they make the entry pass worth watching.

The perimeter has changed, too. Parker is back after an injury-limited season; Hunter Adam brings three years at Davidson; Isaac arrives from Tarleton State. Portland’s current game notes describe an international roster of eleven players, including five Australians. That reach is supported by staff connections: Anthony Separovich’s appointment announcement details his work helping Australian players earn American college places. Recruiting creates options; practice must turn them into a team.

The first four games offer both encouragement and a warning. The Willamette opener brought school records of 133 points and 35 assists. Bethesda provided another comfortable win. Those opponents make the scoring average less revealing than it looks. UT Arlington’s 89–85 victory exposed the other side: Portland committed 21 turnovers and missed eleven free throws. RJ Nance Jr.’s 25 points could not rescue those lost chances.

Keeping players available is central to that effort. Jeff Faraudo’s WCC preview highlights Legans’s concern with health and his appreciation for experienced newcomers. Depth changes what a coach can ask for: harder defensive possessions, more movement and shorter rests before another demanding stretch. Parker’s return also restores a voice in the huddle. Legans has praised his leadership; his value should show in how the group responds after mistakes as well as in his shooting.

That is a useful way to judge substitutions tonight. When a new guard enters, notice who brings the ball up and who takes the toughest defensive assignment. When O’Donnell rests, watch whether the next group still looks inside or changes its spacing. A productive bench does more than preserve a lead. It lets the team keep recognizable habits while different players carry them.

Tonight’s opponent, East Texas A&M, gives Legans another chance to see which habits are taking hold. From your seat, follow a player for three possessions without following the ball. Does he screen, cut, recover on defense or find someone to box out? Then watch the first pass after a rebound and the decision after a defender closes out. Those small actions help explain whether Portland’s offense is becoming dependable. The Hawaii win suggests this team can endure an uncomfortable stretch. The next step is making sound possessions ordinary enough that it does not always need a rescue.

The recruiting strategy

Portland’s international roster reflects a recruiting plan, not a coincidence. The university counts eleven international players this season, including five Australians. That reach gives Shantay Legans access to prospects beyond the crowded domestic market, while transfers bring experience from other American colleges.

The infrastructure matters. Assistant Anthony Separovich previously directed recruiting at AUSA Hoops, helping more than one hundred athletes secure American college placements over three years. Those relationships strengthen Portland’s ability to identify talent and help players navigate the move.

The basketball connection is fit. Legans wants quick passing, pace and perimeter shooting; this roster combines Parker’s shooting, Adam’s experience and the interior work of O’Donnell and George. Recruiting internationally expands the pool from which those complementary skills can be assembled.

Early returns are encouraging: O’Donnell earned Rainbow Classic MVP as Portland opened 3–1. But nationality does not cause wins. Health, defensive execution, development and continuity must turn recruiting connections into reliable basketball. The international pipeline is a strong foundation for that work, rather than a guarantee of success when conference opponents arrive later this winter.
