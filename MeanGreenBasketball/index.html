THE SEASON / NOVEMBER 13, 2026

The next pass.

Daniyal Robinson has more ways to score. Can the Mean Green make their good habits last?

Caps Unlocked · Analysis based on the linked reporting below · Pregame edition ·

North Texas did not give Daniyal Robinson a gentle first year. He arrived in Denton in April 2025 to rebuild a roster from scratch, then lost a leading scorer to a season-ending injury four games into the season. His official biography records the result: 19 wins, a 9–9 conference record and a place in the American tournament quarterfinals. Sixteen of those 33 games were decided by four points or fewer. Robinson’s second season begins with a little continuity and plenty of reasons to value an ordinary, well-executed possession.

The opening three games have offered very different tests. Southwestern Christian was overmatched in a 122–50 opener. McNeese brought a more demanding game and a 77–67 UNT win. Santa Clara left the Super Pit with a 76–71 victory after North Texas stopped making field goals over the final eight minutes. The record is 2–1. The useful question is what the Mean Green can carry from one kind of evening into the next.

Je’Shawn Stevenson is the easiest place to start. American Conference coaches named him preseason player of the year after he averaged 17.3 points in 2025–26. Before Denton, he was the Horizon League’s freshman of the year at Cleveland State. Before that, he became Lindblom’s career scoring leader in Chicago. There is a scoring story at every stop, but this season asks him to make the surrounding players harder to guard, too.

Against McNeese, he did both. UNT’s report of his career-best 30 points also records five assists and five rebounds. Cash Chavis supplied a second-half contribution from the backcourt, while Ousmane Ndour worked on the glass. From the stands, the pass after a defender commits to Stevenson may be as revealing as the shot Stevenson takes himself. A strong scorer creates advantages; a dependable offense knows how to use them.

Ndour gives the season another center of attention. The Senegalese forward arrived through Spain and redshirted last year. Now the missed shot is often the beginning of his work. Through three games, the official cumulative table credits him with eleven offensive rebounds and an 80 percent field-goal mark. Both numbers come from a small sample. Watch the position he establishes before the ball reaches the rim: the useful action often happens before the rebound becomes his.

The Santa Clara recap puts the next challenge plainly. Ndour scored 18 and Reece Robinson added 15, but North Texas could not find a field goal during the closing eight minutes. More scoring options did not automatically produce a finish. That is a reason to study the next late-game possession, rather than assume one November loss defines the team: where does the first action send the defense, and what happens if the intended shot disappears?

The staff is built from relationships as well as résumés. Johnson, Payne, Parker and Ba all worked with Robinson at Cleveland State. Cole Christian arrived this summer with offensive coordination and scouting experience. His hiring announcement traces stops at Iowa State, Akron, Murray State and South Dakota State. Robinson praised his work ethic and basketball mind. The practical job is to turn that accumulated knowledge into decisions a player can make under pressure.

Saturday brings South Alabama, with tip scheduled for 5 p.m. Central. The official two-game preview recalls UNT’s one-point win in Mobile last season and identifies Ndour’s offensive rebounding as an early strength. For a family watching together, choose one task: follow the screener, count the defenders pulled toward a drive, or identify who boxes out. Robinson’s team already has a recognizable scorer. The season’s next answers may come from the players doing something useful while everyone else watches him.

04 / THE BIGGER PICTURE

Built in Denton.

North Texas competes in the American Conference. Daniyal Robinson enters his second season after a 19–14 debut. Through Nov. 11, UNT is 2–1, averaging 90.0 points and allowing 64.3.

International routes

Amar Amkou and Ousmane Ndour bring Senegalese roots, while Filip Kalajzic arrives with Croatian and Slovenian club experience. These routes complement Texas recruits and transfers familiar with Robinson’s system. International recruitment adds different development backgrounds; success still depends on fit, health, defense and execution.
