Eating alongside the novel

Make two dishes from the book at home, or share a dumpling dinner and talk about what Aoyama and Chi-chan notice at the table.

A modern restaurant outing

A table at Din Tai Fung

Din Tai Fung opened as a cooking-oil shop in 1958, after the novel’s 1930s setting. Bring the book to dinner and read the Chapter II noodle scene as you wait for the first steamer.

What to share

  1. Start with pork xiaolongbao. Set a dumpling on a spoon, let it cool briefly, and open the wrapper to sip the broth. Try the ginger and vinegar with the next bite.
  2. Add spicy shrimp and pork wontons, egg fried rice, and a plate of garlic greens. For a smaller table, start with the dumplings, rice, and greens.
  3. Finish with a red bean bun, or try chocolate and mochi xiao long bao from the U.S. menu.

Where to go

Taipei. The original Xinyi shop at No. 194 is takeout-only. Try nearby Xinsheng for a sit-down meal, or Taipei 101 if you’re already visiting the tower. Look up branch waits on the official Taiwan site before heading over.

Portland area. Book at Washington Square in Tigard or Pioneer Place downtown. Check the U.S. menu for the dishes you want to share.

Cook along · Chapter II · p. 36

Bí-thai-bak 米篩目

Look for thick rice sieve noodles sold as mi tai mu (米苔目). For two bowls, brown 4 ounces of minced pork, add 3 cups of stock, and simmer for 5 minutes. Prepare 8 ounces of noodles according to the package, then divide them between the bowls and ladle over the broth. Finish with fried shallots and chopped chives.

For a sweet tasting, chill a small portion of the cooked noodles and serve with rock sugar syrup and ice.

Chapter-title dishChapter VI

Tang-Kue-Tê 冬瓜茶

Winter melon tea. Aoyama is served a glass over ice at the railway hotel in Tainan.

Winter melon and sugar are cooked down into a concentrate, sometimes molded into blocks. Diluted with water and chilled, it becomes an amber drink with a cooked-sugar flavor.
